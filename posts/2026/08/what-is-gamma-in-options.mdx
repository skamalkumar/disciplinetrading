## What Is Gamma in Options?

Gamma is an option Greek that measures how quickly an option's Delta changes when the underlying asset moves.

If Delta tells you the current directional sensitivity of an option, Gamma tells you how quickly that sensitivity can change.

This makes Gamma especially important for short-dated options and positions near the current underlying price.

For the broader picture, start with [What Are Option Greeks?](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-are-option-greeks).

## Gamma and Delta

Consider an option with Delta of 0.50.

If the option has positive Gamma, a rise in the underlying may increase its Delta, while a fall may decrease its Delta.

So Delta is not static.

Gamma measures this change.

A simple way to remember it is:

**Delta = sensitivity to the underlying**

**Gamma = sensitivity of Delta**

## Why Gamma Matters

Gamma becomes important when the underlying makes a rapid move.

A position with significant Gamma can experience a rapid change in directional exposure.

For an option buyer, positive Gamma can be valuable when the underlying makes a large move in the expected direction.

For an option seller, Gamma can create increasing directional risk when the underlying moves quickly toward or through the strike.

## Gamma Near Expiry

Gamma is often particularly significant for options that are close to the money and close to expiry.

As expiry approaches, a relatively small move in the underlying can cause a substantial change in the option's Delta.

This is one reason short-dated options can behave very differently from longer-dated options.

Time decay is also important during this period. See [What Is Time Decay in Options?](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-time-decay-in-options).

## Gamma and ATM Options

Gamma tends to be most relevant around the at-the-money region.

If NIFTY is trading close to a particular strike, a small move around that strike can cause the option's Delta to change quickly.

If you are unfamiliar with these terms, read [What Are ITM, ATM and OTM Options?](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-are-itm-atm-otm-options).

## Gamma for Option Buyers

Option buyers generally benefit from positive Gamma because a favourable underlying move can increase Delta.

As a call moves further into the money, for example, its Delta can increase.

However, buyers still face Theta and may lose money if the expected move does not happen quickly enough.

## Gamma for Option Sellers

Option sellers need to pay particular attention to Gamma risk.

A short option position can become increasingly directional as the underlying moves.

This is one reason sellers must not judge risk simply by looking at the premium received.

## Gamma and Option Strategies

Long options generally have positive Gamma, while short options generally have negative Gamma.

Multi-leg strategies can combine positions so that the overall Gamma is different from the Gamma of any single option.

This is why the complete position should be analysed rather than one leg in isolation.

## Gamma and Volatility

Gamma and implied volatility are different concepts.

Gamma describes how Delta changes with the underlying price.

Vega describes how the option premium responds to changes in implied volatility.

Learn more in [What Is Implied Volatility?](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-implied-volatility).

## Gamma and Option Chain Analysis

Gamma can add another layer to traditional option-chain measures such as Open Interest and volume.

[What Is Open Interest?](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-open-interest) explains outstanding contracts, while [What Is Option Volume?](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-option-volume) explains trading activity.

Gamma instead focuses on how the option's directional sensitivity may change.

## How Beginners Can Think About Gamma

Use this framework:

1. Check the option's Delta.
2. Check its Gamma.
3. Identify how close the strike is to the underlying price.
4. Check time remaining until expiry.
5. Consider Theta and Vega.
6. Evaluate the complete strategy risk.

## Final Thoughts

Gamma helps explain why Delta can change rapidly, particularly around the money and close to expiry.

For beginners, the key idea is simple:

**Delta tells you how much an option responds now. Gamma tells you how quickly that response can change.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does Gamma measure?

Gamma measures the rate of change of an option's Delta as the underlying asset moves.

### Why is Gamma important near expiry?

Near expiry, options close to the money can experience rapid changes in Delta, making Gamma risk more significant.

### Do option buyers have positive Gamma?

Long options generally have positive Gamma, while short options generally have negative Gamma.
