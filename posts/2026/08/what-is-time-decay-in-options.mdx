## What Is Time Decay in Options?

Time decay is the gradual reduction in the time value of an option as it gets closer to expiry, assuming other factors remain unchanged.

In options trading, time is valuable because an option still has time for the underlying asset to make a favourable move.

As expiry approaches, that remaining time becomes smaller.

This is commonly represented by the Greek **Theta**.

To understand the broader components of an option's value, read [What Is Intrinsic Value and Time Value in Options?](https://www.disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-intrinsic-and-time-value).

## Why Does Time Decay Happen?

An option has a limited amount of time in which it can become more valuable.

A long-dated option has more time for the underlying price to move.

A short-dated option has less time.

As expiry approaches, the probability of the option gaining additional time value generally declines.

This is the basic idea behind time decay.

## Time Value and Option Premium

Option premium can broadly be understood as consisting of intrinsic value and time value.

For an option that is out of the money, the premium may consist primarily of time value.

As expiry approaches, this time value can decline rapidly.

## What Is Theta?

Theta is the option Greek used to describe the sensitivity of an option's price to the passage of time, all else being equal.

For a long option position, Theta is generally negative.

This means that, with other variables unchanged, the option tends to lose value as time passes.

For a short option position, the position has the opposite time-decay exposure.

## Why Time Decay Usually Accelerates Near Expiry

Time decay is not necessarily linear.

An option with several weeks remaining has substantial time left.

As expiry gets closer, the remaining time becomes increasingly limited, and the time-value component can decline more quickly, particularly for options near the relevant strike.

This is one reason short-dated options can behave very differently from longer-dated options.

## Example of Time Decay

Imagine an option trading at ₹150 with:

- Intrinsic value: ₹80
- Time value: ₹70

If the underlying price does not move and other conditions remain unchanged, the time-value portion can decline as expiry approaches.

The option could therefore become less valuable even though the underlying asset has not changed much.

This illustrates an important principle:

**An option does not need the underlying price to move against you to lose value.**

Time itself can work against a long option position.

## Time Decay for Option Buyers

Option buyers generally need the underlying asset to move sufficiently and/or quickly enough to offset the erosion of time value.

A correct directional view is therefore not always enough.

If a trader buys a Call expecting the underlying to rise, but the underlying rises too slowly, the option may still lose value because time decay and other pricing factors affect the premium.

## Time Decay for Option Sellers

Option sellers generally benefit from the passage of time when other factors remain favourable.

If an option expires worthless, the seller keeps the premium received, subject to the strategy's other risks.

However, selling options is not simply a way to collect free time decay. Large underlying moves, changes in implied volatility, and expiry risk can produce significant losses.

## Time Decay and Implied Volatility

Time decay does not operate independently.

Option premium is also affected by implied volatility.

If implied volatility rises, an option's premium can increase even while time passes.

If implied volatility falls, premium can decline.

## Time Decay and Moneyness

Time decay can differ depending on whether an option is:

- In the money
- At the money
- Out of the money

The relationship between time value, strike price, and the underlying price is therefore important.

## Time Decay Near Expiry

As expiry approaches, the amount of time remaining becomes extremely small.

For traders holding short-dated options, this means premium can change quickly.

For option buyers, rapid time decay can become a major consideration.

For option sellers, the same mechanism can potentially benefit the position if the underlying remains within the expected range, but the risk of a sharp market move remains.

## How Beginners Can Think About Theta

A simple way to think about Theta is:

**Time is valuable to an option buyer, but it is consumed as expiry approaches.**

For an option seller, the passage of time can work in their favour when the position remains otherwise stable.

This does not mean that every option seller makes money or every option buyer loses money. Price movement, implied volatility, strike selection, and position management all matter.

## Common Mistakes About Time Decay

### Mistake 1: Assuming Every Option Loses the Same Amount Every Day

Time decay varies with the option's characteristics and remaining time.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring Expiry

The same option can behave very differently with 30 days remaining versus one day remaining.

### Mistake 3: Looking Only at Direction

Being right about direction does not automatically mean an option trade will be profitable.

### Mistake 4: Treating Theta as Guaranteed Profit

Theta exposure does not remove directional or volatility risk.

## Final Thoughts

Time decay is one of the most important concepts in options trading.

It explains why the passage of time can reduce an option's time value and why expiry has such a strong influence on option premiums.

Understanding Theta helps traders evaluate whether a strategy is being helped or hurt by the passage of time.
