# What Is a Bonus Issue?

A **bonus issue** is when a company distributes additional shares to eligible existing shareholders by capitalising eligible reserves, subject to applicable rules. The number of shares increases, but the market price per share generally adjusts.

## Example: 1:1 bonus issue

Suppose you own 100 shares priced at ₹500 each, worth ₹50,000. In a 1:1 bonus issue, you receive one additional share for every share held.

| Measure | Before | Theoretical after adjustment |
|---|---:|---:|
| Shares held | 100 | 200 |
| Price per share | ₹500 | ₹250 |
| Holding value | ₹50,000 | ₹50,000 |

This illustration ignores market movements. The actual price may rise or fall after the event.

## Why might a company issue bonus shares?

- Capitalise eligible reserves.
- Increase the number of shares in circulation.
- Reduce the nominal price per share.
- Signal management confidence, though the action alone does not prove strong fundamentals.

## Bonus issue vs stock split

A bonus issue creates additional shares from eligible reserves under the applicable corporate-action framework. A stock split subdivides existing shares. Both generally reduce the theoretical per-share price and neither automatically increases total economic value.

## What investors should evaluate

Review revenue growth, profitability, free cash flow, debt, return on capital, and valuation. Confirm the ratio, record date, and eligibility from the company's official filing.

## Key takeaway

More shares do not automatically mean more wealth. The business and market value matter more than the share count alone.

## FAQs

### Are bonus shares free?
No additional purchase payment is generally required to receive eligible shares, but the per-share price usually adjusts. The action does not create extra value by itself.

### Does a bonus issue improve EPS?
The share count used in EPS calculations changes, and per-share figures may be adjusted under accounting standards. The bonus issue itself does not increase total earnings.

### Should I buy before a bonus issue?
Do not invest solely to receive bonus shares. Consider fundamentals, valuation, risks, and your objectives.
