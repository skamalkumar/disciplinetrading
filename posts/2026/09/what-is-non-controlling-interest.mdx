# What Is Non-Controlling Interest?

**Non-Controlling Interest (NCI)** represents the portion of a controlled subsidiary owned by shareholders other than the parent company. It is also commonly called minority interest.

## Example

A parent owns 80% of a subsidiary with net assets of ₹500 crore.

A simplified NCI calculation is:

**20% × ₹500 crore = ₹100 crore**

Actual acquisition accounting can be more detailed.

## NCI in Consolidated Statements

When a parent controls a subsidiary, consolidated statements generally include the subsidiary's assets, liabilities, income and expenses.

NCI identifies the portion attributable to outside shareholders.

Suppose the subsidiary earns ₹100 crore and the parent owns 80%.

A simplified allocation is:

- Parent owners = ₹80 crore
- NCI = ₹20 crore

## NCI and Equity

NCI is generally presented separately within consolidated equity.

Suppose:

- Consolidated equity = ₹5,000 crore
- Equity attributable to parent = ₹4,200 crore
- NCI = ₹800 crore

An investor analysing value belonging specifically to parent shareholders should distinguish these amounts.

## NCI and Enterprise Value

NCI can matter when comparing enterprise value with consolidated EBITDA because consolidated operating figures can include controlled subsidiaries that are not wholly owned.

A simplified bridge can be:

**EV = Equity Value + Debt + NCI − Cash**

Example:

₹10,000 crore + ₹2,000 crore + ₹600 crore − ₹500 crore = **₹12,100 crore**

Actual valuation may require additional adjustments.

## Key Takeaways

- NCI represents outside ownership in controlled subsidiaries.
- It is also called minority interest.
- NCI is equity, not debt.
- It matters when interpreting consolidated profit, equity and valuation.

## FAQs

### Is NCI debt?
No. It is an equity interest.

### Why does NCI matter in valuation?
Consolidated operating numbers may include businesses that are not 100% owned.

### Can NCI be significant?
Yes, depending on subsidiary size and ownership.

## Final Thoughts

NCI helps investors understand **which part of a consolidated group's economics belongs to parent shareholders and which part belongs to outside owners**.
