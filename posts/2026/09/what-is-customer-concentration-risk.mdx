## What Is Customer Concentration Risk?

A company can report impressive growth while depending heavily on a handful of customers. This creates customer concentration risk: the possibility that losing one or a few major customers materially harms revenue, cash flow or operations.

## Practical Example

Suppose a supplier earns ₹1,000 crore in annual revenue, of which ₹350 crore comes from its largest customer. If that customer reduces orders by half, the supplier could lose ₹175 crore of revenue before considering replacement business or cost adjustments. The effect on profit may be larger if costs are fixed.

## How Investors Can Analyse It

Analysts can examine customer disclosures, segment notes, contract duration, renewal terms, receivables concentration and management commentary. A high concentration is not automatically bad: long contracts, high switching costs and strong customer relationships may reduce risk. But concentration deserves a margin of safety and scenario analysis.

## Important Interpretation

Revenue concentration is different from industry concentration. Several customers in one sector can all be affected by the same cycle. Investors should assess both customer-specific dependence and common end-market exposure. A useful checklist is to identify disclosed top-customer shares, assess contract durability, estimate replacement time, review payment behavior and stress-test a meaningful customer loss. Lack of disclosure should be treated as uncertainty—not proof of low concentration.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is this metric or concept enough to make an investment decision?

No. It should be considered alongside business fundamentals, financial statements, valuation, governance, risks and the investor’s own circumstances.

### What should an investor do if disclosure is limited?

Treat the missing information as uncertainty, avoid unsupported assumptions and seek corroborating information in company filings and reliable disclosures.

### Does this concept apply to Indian listed companies?

Yes, as an analytical concept. Investors should consult the company’s latest filings and applicable exchange and regulatory disclosures for current details.
