# What Is Shareholders' Equity?

**Shareholders' Equity** is the accounting value attributable to a company's owners after liabilities are deducted from assets.

A simple question explains it:

> What remains for shareholders after the company's liabilities are deducted from its assets?

## Shareholders' Equity Formula

The accounting equation is:

**Assets = Liabilities + Equity**

Therefore:

**Shareholders' Equity = Total Assets − Total Liabilities**

Suppose:

- Total assets = ₹10,000 crore
- Total liabilities = ₹6,000 crore

Shareholders' equity = **₹4,000 crore**

## What Does Shareholders' Equity Include?

Depending on the company's structure and accounting framework, equity can include:

- Share capital
- Securities premium
- Retained earnings
- Other reserves
- Other equity components
- Relevant treasury-share adjustments

The exact presentation varies across companies.

## Shareholders' Equity vs Market Capitalization

These are different concepts.

**Shareholders' Equity** is an accounting balance-sheet measure.

**Market Capitalization** is the market value of the company's shares.

For example:

- Shareholders' equity = ₹4,000 crore
- Market capitalization = ₹12,000 crore

The market can value the company above or below its accounting equity.

## Equity and Book Value

A simplified book value per share is:

**Book Value Per Share = Equity attributable to ordinary shareholders ÷ Ordinary shares**

Suppose:

- Equity = ₹4,000 crore
- Shares = 100 crore

Book value per share = **₹40**

## Equity and ROE

A simplified ROE formula is:

**ROE = Net Profit ÷ Average Shareholders' Equity × 100**

Suppose:

- Net profit = ₹600 crore
- Average equity = ₹3,000 crore

ROE = **20%**

This means the company generated ₹20 of profit for every ₹100 of average equity under this simplified calculation.

## How Equity Changes

Shareholders' equity can change because of:

- Profits
- Losses
- Dividends
- New share issuance
- Share buybacks
- Reserve movements
- Other comprehensive income

Example:

- Beginning equity = ₹5,000 crore
- Net profit = ₹700 crore
- Dividend = ₹200 crore

Ignoring other changes:

Ending equity ≈ **₹5,500 crore**

## Equity and Debt

Companies can finance themselves through equity, debt or both.

| Company | Equity | Debt |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | ₹5,000 cr | ₹1,000 cr |
| B | ₹2,000 cr | ₹5,000 cr |

Company B uses substantially more debt relative to equity.

But the appropriate capital structure depends on industry economics, cash flow and risk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is shareholders' equity the same as share capital?

No. Share capital is one component of shareholders' equity.

### Is shareholders' equity the same as market value?

No. Equity is an accounting measure, while market capitalization is the market value assigned by investors.

### Can shareholders' equity be negative?

Yes. Accumulated losses and other adjustments can result in negative equity.

### Why does equity matter for ROE?

ROE measures profit relative to equity, so changes in equity directly affect the ratio.

## Final Thoughts

Shareholders' equity is a foundation of balance-sheet analysis.

**Equity = Assets − Liabilities**

But investors should go further: examine how equity changes, how much debt is used, whether profits are retained, and what return the company earns on shareholder capital.
