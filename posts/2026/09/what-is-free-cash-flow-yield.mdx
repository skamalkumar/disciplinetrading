## What Is Free Cash Flow Yield?

**Free Cash Flow Yield (FCF Yield)** compares a company's free cash flow with its market capitalization.

It answers:

> How much free cash flow is the business generating relative to the price investors are paying for the equity?

## Formula

**FCF Yield = Free Cash Flow ÷ Market Capitalization × 100**

A common definition of Free Cash Flow is:

**Operating Cash Flow − Capital Expenditure**

Use a consistent definition when comparing companies.

## Example

Suppose:

- Free Cash Flow = ₹500 crore
- Market Capitalization = ₹5,000 crore

FCF Yield = ₹500 ÷ ₹5,000 × 100 = **10%**

## FCF Yield vs Earnings Yield

Earnings Yield generally uses accounting earnings:

**Earnings Yield = Earnings ÷ Market Capitalization × 100**

FCF Yield uses free cash flow instead.

Suppose:

- Earnings = ₹400 crore
- Free Cash Flow = ₹500 crore
- Market Cap = ₹5,000 crore

Earnings Yield = **8%**

FCF Yield = **10%**

## Indian Equity Example

| Metric | Company A | Company B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Market Cap | ₹10,000 crore | ₹10,000 crore |
| Free Cash Flow | ₹800 crore | ₹300 crore |
| FCF Yield | 8% | 3% |

Company A generates more FCF relative to market value.

But investors should investigate why.

Company B may be investing heavily in new capacity, which could affect future cash generation.

## High FCF Yield: Cheap or a Warning?

A high FCF Yield can indicate attractive valuation, but it can also occur because of:

- Temporary cash-flow spikes
- Cyclical peak conditions
- Underinvestment
- Weak growth expectations
- Business deterioration

Therefore, high yield alone is not a buy signal.

## FCF Quality

Investors should examine:

- Operating cash flow
- Capital expenditure
- Receivables
- Working capital
- Business cyclicality
- One-off cash benefits

## Advantages

- Cash-based valuation perspective
- Complements P/E and Earnings Yield
- Useful for peer comparison
- Connects valuation with cash generation

## Limitations

- FCF can be volatile
- Capital expenditure can vary
- Cyclical companies can distort the ratio
- Different FCF definitions can produce different results

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is high FCF Yield good?

It can indicate strong cash generation relative to valuation, but sustainability must be checked.

### Can FCF Yield be negative?

Yes. Negative free cash flow produces a negative FCF Yield.

## Final Thoughts

FCF Yield connects **cash generation with stock valuation**.

The best opportunity is not necessarily the company with the highest yield, but one where free cash flow is sustainable and the valuation is reasonable.
