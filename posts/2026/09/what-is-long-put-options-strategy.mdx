# What Is Long Put Strategy?

A long Put means buying a Put for bearish exposure or to hedge an existing position. The buyer pays a premium for the right to sell at the strike under the contract terms.

## How It Works

Suppose NIFTY is 22,500 and a trader buys a 22,400 Put for 90 points. If NIFTY expires at 22,200, intrinsic value is 200 and profit before costs is 110 points. If it expires above 22,400, the premium may be lost.

## Risk and Payoff

Maximum loss is the premium paid. Expiry breakeven is strike minus premium: 22,310 in this example. A Put can lose value despite a small market decline if time decay or falling implied volatility outweighs the directional move. A portfolio hedge may not track the portfolio perfectly.

## Practical Checklist

Before placing a trade, calculate the payoff at several expiry prices using live premiums. Include lot size, brokerage, taxes, bid-ask spread and slippage. Define the maximum acceptable loss and the conditions that would invalidate the trade. Do not rely on premium received as evidence that a trade is safe.

## Final Thoughts

A long Put can provide bearish exposure or a defined-premium hedge. Its value depends on the magnitude and timing of the decline, volatility and remaining time.
