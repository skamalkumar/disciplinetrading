## What Is Debt-to-Capital Ratio?

The **Debt-to-Capital Ratio** measures how much of a company's defined capital structure is funded by debt.

A common simplified formula is:

**Debt-to-Capital Ratio = Debt ÷ (Debt + Shareholders' Equity) × 100**

In simple terms:

**What percentage of debt-and-equity capital comes from debt?**

## Simple Example

Suppose:

- Debt = ₹400 crore
- Shareholders' equity = ₹600 crore

Total capital:

**₹400 + ₹600 = ₹1,000 crore**

Debt-to-Capital:

**₹400 ÷ ₹1,000 × 100 = 40%**

## Debt-to-Capital vs Debt-to-Equity

These ratios are related but different.

**Debt-to-Equity = Debt ÷ Equity**

**Debt-to-Capital = Debt ÷ (Debt + Equity)**

If debt is ₹400 crore and equity is ₹600 crore:

- Debt-to-Equity = 0.67×
- Debt-to-Capital = 40%

## Why Does It Matter?

Debt can finance:

- Expansion
- Acquisitions
- New capacity
- Working capital
- Capital expenditure

But debt also creates interest and repayment obligations.

Debt-to-Capital helps investors understand the role of borrowing in the company's capital structure.

## NIFTY Example

Consider two hypothetical NIFTY companies:

| Metric | Company A | Company B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Debt | ₹1,000 cr | ₹2,000 cr |
| Equity | ₹2,000 cr | ₹2,000 cr |
| Debt-to-Capital | 33.3% | 50% |

The second company has a larger debt component relative to its defined capital base.

The next step is to examine cash flow and debt-servicing capacity.

## Debt-to-Capital and Interest Coverage

[What Is Interest Coverage Ratio?](/blog/what-is-interest-coverage-ratio) measures operating earnings relative to interest expense.

Debt-to-Capital measures capital structure.

They therefore answer different questions.

A company can have a moderate debt percentage but weak interest coverage if earnings are low.

## Debt-to-Capital and Cash Flow

Debt should be analysed alongside:

- Operating cash flow
- Free cash flow
- Interest expense
- Debt maturities
- Capital expenditure
- Working capital

See [What Is Free Cash Flow?](/blog/what-is-free-cash-flow).

## How Investors Can Use It

1. Define debt consistently.
2. Calculate the ratio.
3. Review the multi-year trend.
4. Compare with similar companies.
5. Check interest coverage.
6. Review operating and free cash flow.
7. Examine debt maturities.
8. Understand why leverage changed.

## Limitations

- Debt definitions can differ.
- Equity changes over time.
- Industry capital structures vary.
- The ratio does not measure cash-flow capacity by itself.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the formula?

**Debt ÷ (Debt + Equity) × 100.**

### Is it the same as Debt-to-Equity?

No. Debt-to-Equity compares debt directly with equity.

### Is lower always better?

Not necessarily. The appropriate capital structure depends on the business model, cash flows and industry.

## Final Thoughts

Debt-to-Capital Ratio provides a useful view of how debt participates in a company's capital structure.

Combine it with **interest coverage, cash flow, debt maturities, profitability and business context**.
