## What Is Cash Ratio?

The **Cash Ratio** measures a company's ability to meet current liabilities using cash, cash equivalents and qualifying short-term marketable securities.

It is one of the most conservative liquidity ratios.

## Formula

**Cash Ratio = (Cash + Cash Equivalents + Marketable Securities) ÷ Current Liabilities**

Unlike the Current Ratio and Quick Ratio, it generally does not rely on inventory sales or collection of receivables.

## Example

Suppose:

- Cash = ₹80 crore
- Short-term investments = ₹20 crore
- Current liabilities = ₹100 crore

Cash Ratio = (₹80 + ₹20) ÷ ₹100 = **1.00**

The company has ₹1 of cash-like assets for every ₹1 of current liabilities.

## Cash Ratio vs Quick Ratio

| Ratio | Main Assets |
|---|---|
| Current Ratio | Broad current assets |
| Quick Ratio | Liquid assets, generally excluding inventory |
| Cash Ratio | Cash, cash equivalents and qualifying securities |

A company can therefore have a strong Current Ratio while having a much lower Cash Ratio.

## Is a High Cash Ratio Good?

A high ratio generally means stronger immediate liquidity.

But extremely high cash balances are not automatically positive. Excess cash may remain idle instead of being invested, returned to shareholders or used to reduce debt.

## Is a Low Cash Ratio Bad?

Not necessarily.

Businesses with predictable operating cash flows can operate with low cash balances because they continuously collect receivables and generate cash from operations.

## Indian Equity Example

| Metric | Company A | Company B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash + Securities | ₹500 crore | ₹100 crore |
| Current Liabilities | ₹500 crore | ₹500 crore |
| Cash Ratio | 1.00 | 0.20 |

Company A has stronger immediate liquidity, but the investor should also examine cash flow, debt and business stability.

## Industry Matters

There is no universal ideal Cash Ratio.

Compare companies with similar business models and examine the company's historical trend.

## Advantages

- Conservative liquidity measure
- Easy to understand
- Useful during financial stress
- Helps assess immediate balance-sheet flexibility

## Limitations

- Does not measure profitability
- Does not measure future cash generation
- Industry norms vary
- Excess cash can reduce capital efficiency

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a Cash Ratio of 1 good?

It means qualifying cash-like assets approximately equal current liabilities. Whether that is appropriate depends on the business.

### Is Cash Ratio better than Quick Ratio?

It is more conservative, but each ratio answers a different question.

### Can a low Cash Ratio be acceptable?

Yes. Strong operating cash generation can make a lower ratio perfectly normal.

## Final Thoughts

Cash Ratio gives investors a focused view of **immediate liquidity**.

Use it with Current Ratio, Quick Ratio, debt and cash-flow analysis.
