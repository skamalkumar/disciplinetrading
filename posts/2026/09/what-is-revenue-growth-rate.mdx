# What Is Revenue Growth Rate?

**Revenue growth rate** measures how quickly a company's sales or operating revenue is increasing or decreasing over a period.

## Formula

**Revenue Growth Rate = (Current Revenue − Previous Revenue) ÷ Previous Revenue × 100**

## Example

Previous revenue = ₹1,000 crore.

Current revenue = ₹1,200 crore.

Growth:

**(1,200 − 1,000) ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 20%**

## NIFTY-Style Example

| Year | Revenue |
|---|---:|
| FY2024 | ₹8,000 Cr |
| FY2025 | ₹9,200 Cr |
| FY2026 | ₹11,040 Cr |

Growth was 15% and then 20%, showing acceleration.

## Revenue Growth vs Profit Growth

Revenue growth does not automatically mean profit growth.

If revenue rises 15% but costs rise faster, margins can decline.

Therefore examine revenue alongside:

- operating margin,
- EBITDA,
- EBIT,
- net profit,
- EPS, and
- operating cash flow.

## CAGR

For multiple years:

**CAGR = (Ending Revenue ÷ Beginning Revenue)^(1/n) − 1**

CAGR helps investors understand the compound pace of growth rather than focusing on a single year.

## Quality of Growth

Ask why revenue is increasing:

- higher volumes,
- price increases,
- new products,
- new markets,
- acquisitions, or
- currency effects.

Also check whether receivables, debt and cash flow are behaving sensibly.

## Warning Signs

Be cautious when revenue rises but:

- receivables grow much faster,
- cash flow does not follow,
- margins deteriorate,
- debt rises rapidly, or
- growth depends heavily on acquisitions.

## Final Thoughts

Good analysis asks three questions:

**How fast is revenue growing?**

**Why is it growing?**

**Is the growth translating into profit and cash flow?**
