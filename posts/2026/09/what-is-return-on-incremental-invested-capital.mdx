## What Is Return on Incremental Invested Capital?

Return on Incremental Invested Capital (incremental ROIC) estimates the operating return generated by additional capital invested over a period. It can help investors evaluate whether a company’s growth is creating value, rather than relying only on returns from its existing asset base.

## Practical Example

In a simplified example, operating NOPAT rises from ₹100 crore to ₹120 crore, while invested capital rises from ₹500 crore to ₹600 crore. The incremental return is ₹20 crore divided by ₹100 crore, or 20%. This result depends on consistent definitions and period alignment.

## How Investors Can Analyse It

A common analytical approach is incremental NOPAT divided by incremental invested capital. Use comparable periods, adjust for acquisitions or disposals where material, and investigate volatile working capital or one-off effects. Small or negative changes in capital can make the ratio unstable or meaningless.

## Important Interpretation

A high incremental return can indicate attractive reinvestment opportunities, but it should be assessed over multiple years and alongside growth runway, competition, accounting quality and the cost of capital. It is not a standalone buy signal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is this metric or concept enough to make an investment decision?

No. It should be considered alongside business fundamentals, financial statements, valuation, governance, risks and the investor’s own circumstances.

### What should an investor do if disclosure is limited?

Treat the missing information as uncertainty, avoid unsupported assumptions and seek corroborating information in company filings and reliable disclosures.

### Does this concept apply to Indian listed companies?

Yes, as an analytical concept. Investors should consult the company’s latest filings and applicable exchange and regulatory disclosures for current details.
