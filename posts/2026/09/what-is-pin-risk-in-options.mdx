# What Is Pin Risk in Options?

**Pin risk** is the uncertainty that arises when an option's underlying price finishes very close to its strike near expiry. A small final price movement can change whether an option finishes in or out of the money, potentially creating uncertainty about exercise, assignment, or the position left after expiry.

Pin risk is especially relevant to traders holding short options near expiry. It does not mean the market will deliberately settle at a particular strike.

## How it works

Suppose a trader has sold a NIFTY call with a strike of 22,500. Near expiry, the index is hovering around 22,500. A final move above or below the strike can change the option's moneyness. The exact outcome and settlement process depend on the contract, exchange rules, and broker procedures; never assume all instruments follow identical rules.

## Why it matters

Pin risk can complicate multi-leg strategies. One leg may have a different exercise or settlement outcome than expected, leaving an unintended directional position or margin requirement. Looking only at the combined premium can hide this risk.

## Risk-management checklist

1. Read the current exchange and broker rules for the exact contract.
2. Review each leg of a spread separately.
3. Consider reducing or closing short-option exposure before the final trading window if uncertainty is unacceptable.
4. Do not rely on a last-second quote or an assumption about exercise.
5. Keep sufficient margin and know how the broker handles expiry outcomes.

## Pin risk versus assignment risk

Assignment risk is the possibility that a short option is assigned under the applicable contract rules. Pin risk is specifically the uncertainty when the underlying finishes very close to a strike near expiry. Assignment risk can exist even when the underlying is not near a strike.

## Key takeaway

Expiry is a process, not just a payoff diagram. Understand the contract rules and manage short-option exposure before the final minutes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is pin risk the same as assignment risk?
No. They are related but different. Pin risk concerns uncertainty near the strike at expiry; assignment risk concerns the possibility of assignment.

### Can pin risk affect a spread?
Yes. Different outcomes across legs can leave a position different from what the trader expected.

### How can I reduce pin risk?
Understand contract rules and consider closing or reducing short-option positions before expiry when the remaining uncertainty is not worth the exposure.
