# What Is Capital Reserve?

A **Capital Reserve** is a reserve created from certain capital transactions or capital profits rather than from a company's normal operating profits.

It forms part of shareholders' equity, subject to the applicable accounting framework.

## How Can It Arise?

Depending on the transaction and applicable rules, capital reserves can arise from certain:

- Capital profits
- Capital transactions
- Revaluation-related amounts where permitted
- Other items classified as capital in nature

The precise treatment depends on accounting standards and corporate law.

## Capital Reserve vs Revenue Reserve

| Feature | Capital Reserve | Revenue Reserve |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Capital-type transactions | Business profits |
| Operating profit | Generally no | Generally yes |
| Equity component | Yes | Yes |

A reserve is an accounting/equity classification. It is not a separate bank account.

## Example

Suppose a hypothetical company recognises ₹100 crore in a transaction that is required to be classified as capital in nature and transfers the amount to a capital reserve.

The balance sheet may then show:

**Capital Reserve = ₹100 crore**

This does not mean the company generated ₹100 crore of operating cash.

## Capital Reserve vs Securities Premium

Securities Premium generally arises when shares are issued above face value.

Capital reserve is a broader category for specified capital transactions. The two should not automatically be treated as interchangeable.

## What Investors Should Check

When a capital reserve is material, examine:

1. Its source
2. The accounting note explaining it
3. Whether it represents realised or unrealised amounts
4. Applicable restrictions on its use
5. Whether the company also has sufficient cash and liquid investments

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is this figure the same as cash?

No. An accounting or capital-structure figure should not automatically be interpreted as the company's current cash balance.

### Can it be analysed on its own?

It is better used alongside profitability, cash flow, debt, share count and other relevant measures.
