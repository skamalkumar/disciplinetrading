## What Is the Options Wheel Strategy?

The **options wheel** is a rules-based approach that alternates between selling cash-secured Puts and selling covered Calls. A trader may begin by selling a Put on an asset they are willing and financially able to own. If assigned and shares are acquired, the trader may then sell covered Calls against those shares. If the shares are called away, the cycle can return to selling Puts.

The name “wheel” describes this repeating sequence. It does not mean the strategy produces steady income or avoids losses.

## The Three Stages

### 1. Sell a Cash-Secured Put

The trader sells a Put and keeps sufficient cash or eligible collateral to meet the potential purchase obligation. The premium is received, but the trader may be required to buy the underlying at the strike under the contract's exercise and settlement rules.

### 2. Own the Shares and Sell a Covered Call

If shares are acquired, the trader may sell a Call against the shareholding. The premium provides cash flow, but the shares can fall in value. If the Call is exercised or assigned, the shares may be sold at the strike according to the contract terms.

### 3. Repeat the Cycle

If the shares are called away, the trader can decide whether to sell another cash-secured Put. The decision should depend on valuation, portfolio exposure and current market conditions—not merely on keeping the cycle running.

## Illustrative Example

Assume a stock trades at ₹500. A trader sells a ₹480 Put and receives ₹12 per share. If the option expires without assignment, the premium is retained, before costs. If the trader is required to buy at ₹480, the simple premium-adjusted acquisition cost is ₹468 per share, before costs and taxes.

After acquiring shares, suppose the trader sells a ₹520 Call for ₹10. If the shares are called away at ₹520, the trader receives the strike price for the shares and keeps the Call premium, subject to the actual transaction and settlement mechanics. If the share price falls, the premium may be small compared with the decline in the shareholding.

## What Are the Potential Advantages?

- A systematic framework for entering a share position.
- Premium collection at more than one stage of the cycle.
- A disciplined way to define the price at which the trader is willing to buy or sell.

## The Risks Often Underestimated

- **Downside exposure:** A cash-secured Put does not protect against a large fall in the underlying.
- **Opportunity cost:** Covered Calls can limit gains if the share price rises sharply.
- **Concentration:** Repeatedly wheeling one company can create excessive exposure.
- **Volatility and premium:** High premiums may reflect high expected risk, not an attractive opportunity.
- **Execution and tax:** Brokerage, bid–ask spreads, taxes and assignment/settlement rules affect returns.
- **Capital lock-up:** Cash or shares may remain committed for long periods.

## How to Evaluate the Wheel Properly

Track the complete cycle, not just premiums collected. Include share purchase and sale prices, realised and unrealised share gains or losses, option premiums, fees, taxes, dividends where applicable and the opportunity cost of capital. Compare the result with simply holding the underlying or using a cash alternative.

The wheel is most coherent when the trader genuinely wants to own the underlying at the Put strike and is comfortable selling it at the Call strike. It should not be used to justify holding a fundamentally deteriorating company.

## Wheel Strategy vs Separate Put and Call Trades

The wheel links the two trades into one process. A cash-secured Put and a covered Call can also be used independently. The wheel's distinctive feature is the planned transition from one stage to the next after the underlying position changes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the wheel guarantee monthly income?

No. Premiums are uncertain and can be outweighed by losses in the underlying shares or by the cost of closing options.

### Is the wheel a bullish strategy?

It generally assumes the trader is willing to own the underlying and later sell it at a selected price. It is not a substitute for a clear view of the asset's risk and value.

### Can the wheel be used on index options?

The classic wheel assumes the ability to acquire and hold the underlying shares. Cash-settled index options do not necessarily provide the same share-ownership cycle. Check the specific contract and settlement method.
