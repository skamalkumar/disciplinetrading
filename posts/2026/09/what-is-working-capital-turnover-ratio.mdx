## What Is Working Capital Turnover Ratio?

The **Working Capital Turnover Ratio** measures how efficiently a company uses working capital to generate revenue.

It answers a simple question:

> How much revenue is being generated for each rupee of working capital?

## Formula

**Working Capital Turnover Ratio = Revenue ÷ Average Working Capital**

Working Capital:

**Current Assets − Current Liabilities**

Average Working Capital:

**(Opening Working Capital + Closing Working Capital) ÷ 2**

## Example

Suppose:

- Opening working capital = ₹200 crore
- Closing working capital = ₹250 crore
- Revenue = ₹2,000 crore

Average working capital:

(₹200 + ₹250) ÷ 2 = ₹225 crore

Working Capital Turnover:

₹2,000 ÷ ₹225 = **8.89 times**

The company generated approximately ₹8.89 of revenue for every ₹1 of average working capital.

## What Does a High Ratio Mean?

A higher ratio can indicate efficient use of working capital.

Possible reasons include:

- Fast inventory movement
- Faster receivable collection
- Efficient supplier terms
- Strong sales relative to working-capital needs

But higher is not always better.

An extremely high ratio may indicate that the company has too little working capital to comfortably support operations.

## What Does a Low Ratio Mean?

A low ratio can indicate that substantial capital is tied up relative to revenue.

Possible reasons include:

- Excess inventory
- Slow receivables
- Weak sales
- Large current-asset balances

The reason matters more than the number itself.

## Indian Equity Example

Consider two companies:

| Metric | Company A | Company B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | ₹5,000 crore | ₹5,000 crore |
| Average Working Capital | ₹500 crore | ₹1,000 crore |
| Turnover | 10× | 5× |

Company A generates more revenue per rupee of working capital.

However, Company B may operate in a business requiring larger inventory or longer customer-credit periods.

## Working Capital and Inventory

Inventory can absorb significant operating capital.

If inventory grows faster than sales, working capital can increase and turnover may decline.

## Trend Analysis

Suppose a company reports:

| Year | Ratio |
|---|---:|
| FY2024 | 5.2× |
| FY2025 | 6.8× |
| FY2026 | 8.1× |

The improvement may indicate better working-capital efficiency.

But investors should determine whether the improvement came from genuine operating efficiency or an unusually low working-capital balance.

## Advantages

- Measures working-capital efficiency
- Connects sales with capital requirements
- Useful for historical and peer comparisons
- Helps identify operating changes

## Limitations

- Industry differences are significant
- Seasonal businesses can distort the ratio
- Revenue does not equal cash flow
- A high ratio is not automatically positive

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a good Working Capital Turnover Ratio?

There is no universal ideal. Compare it with industry peers and the company's history.

### Is higher always better?

No. A very high ratio can also mean insufficient working-capital reserves.

## Final Thoughts

Working Capital Turnover Ratio helps equity investors understand how efficiently a business converts operating capital into sales.

Use it alongside liquidity, cash flow, inventory and receivables analysis.
