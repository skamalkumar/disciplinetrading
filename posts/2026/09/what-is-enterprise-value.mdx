## What Is Enterprise Value?

Enterprise Value, commonly called EV, is a valuation measure that provides a broader view of a company's business value by considering equity value, debt and cash.

In simple terms:

**Market Capitalization tells you the value of the equity. Enterprise Value gives a broader view of the business value.**

For the basics of market value, see [What Is Market Capitalization? A Beginner's Guide](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-market-capitalization).

## Enterprise Value Formula

A simplified formula is:

**Enterprise Value = Market Capitalization + Debt − Cash**

Depending on the company and data source, the calculation may include additional adjustments such as preferred stock or minority interests.

## A Simple Example

Suppose a company has:

- Market Capitalization: ₹10,000 crore
- Debt: ₹3,000 crore
- Cash: ₹1,000 crore

Then:

**EV = ₹10,000 + ₹3,000 − ₹1,000 = ₹12,000 crore**

## Why Does Debt Matter?

Two companies can have identical market capitalizations but very different debt levels.

Enterprise Value brings that difference into the valuation framework.

For leverage context, see [What Is Debt-to-Equity Ratio? A Beginner's Guide](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-debt-to-equity-ratio).

## Why Is Cash Subtracted?

Cash is generally subtracted in the simplified EV formula because it is a financial asset held by the company.

Two businesses with identical market capitalization and debt can therefore have different EVs if their cash balances differ.

Cash strength should also be considered alongside [What Is Free Cash Flow? A Beginner's Guide](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-free-cash-flow).

## Enterprise Value vs Market Capitalization

**Market Capitalization:** market value of the company's equity.

**Enterprise Value:** a broader capital-structure-adjusted valuation measure.

Neither tells you by itself whether a stock is cheap or expensive.

EV can also be compared with revenue through EV/Revenue or EV/Sales.

For the underlying sales concept, see [What Is Revenue? A Beginner's Guide to Company Sales and Growth](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-revenue).

The usefulness of such multiples depends on margins, growth and industry economics.

## When Is Enterprise Value Useful?

A disciplined investor can use EV when:

1. Comparing companies with different debt levels.
2. Comparing businesses using EV-based valuation multiples.
3. Studying acquisition-style valuation.
4. Understanding the relationship between equity value and capital structure.
5. Building a broader valuation framework.

## Common Mistakes

### Treating EV as Share Price

EV is a company-level measure, not the price of one share.

### Ignoring Debt and Cash

Both can materially affect enterprise value.

### Assuming Lower EV Means Cheaper

A lower EV does not automatically mean a stock is undervalued.

### Comparing Unrelated Businesses

Valuation measures should generally be compared between relevant peers.

## Final Thoughts

Enterprise Value provides a broader valuation perspective than market capitalization alone.

Used alongside EBITDA, revenue, profitability, cash flow and industry comparisons, EV can help investors understand how the market is valuing an operating business.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Enterprise Value?

Enterprise Value is a valuation measure that broadly combines equity value with debt and subtracts relevant cash, subject to the calculation methodology.

### Is Enterprise Value the same as market capitalization?

No. Market capitalization measures equity value, while EV provides a broader capital-structure-adjusted view.

### Why is cash subtracted from EV?

In the simplified framework, cash is subtracted because it is an asset held by the company.

### Why is EV useful?

It is particularly useful when comparing businesses with different debt and cash positions.
