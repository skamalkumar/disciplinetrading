## CapEx vs Depreciation: What Equity Investors Should Know

Capital expenditure (CapEx) is cash spent to acquire, improve or maintain long-lived assets, subject to accounting treatment. Depreciation allocates the depreciable cost of those assets over their useful lives. They are related, but they are not interchangeable.

## Practical Example

A manufacturer spends ₹100 crore on equipment. The cash outflow is generally reflected in investing cash flows when paid, while depreciation expense is recognized over the asset’s useful life. In a simplified ten-year straight-line example with no residual value, annual depreciation would be ₹10 crore.

## How Investors Can Analyse It

CapEx consistently above depreciation may indicate expansion, inflation, asset replacement timing or a younger asset base. CapEx below depreciation can reflect a mature or asset-light model—or underinvestment. Interpret the pattern with capacity utilization, maintenance needs, asset age and cash generation.

## Important Interpretation

Maintenance and growth CapEx are often useful analytical concepts, but companies do not always disclose them separately. Avoid assuming all CapEx above depreciation is growth spending or that depreciation precisely measures replacement cost.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is this metric or concept enough to make an investment decision?

No. It should be considered alongside business fundamentals, financial statements, valuation, governance, risks and the investor’s own circumstances.

### What should an investor do if disclosure is limited?

Treat the missing information as uncertainty, avoid unsupported assumptions and seek corroborating information in company filings and reliable disclosures.

### Does this concept apply to Indian listed companies?

Yes, as an analytical concept. Investors should consult the company’s latest filings and applicable exchange and regulatory disclosures for current details.
