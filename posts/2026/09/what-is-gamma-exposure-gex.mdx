# What Is Gamma Exposure (GEX)?

**Gamma exposure (GEX)** is a market-analysis concept used to estimate how options-related Gamma may translate into hedging demand as the underlying price changes. Analysts use it to form hypotheses about whether hedging flows might dampen or reinforce price movements.

GEX is not a directly observable universal number. Providers may use different assumptions about positions, contract multipliers, sign conventions, and dealer holdings. Treat a GEX chart as a model, not a guarantee.

## First, understand Gamma

Gamma measures how quickly an option's Delta changes when the underlying price changes. Near-the-money options close to expiry can have particularly sensitive Gamma. If a participant hedges option Delta, changes in Delta may require further buying or selling of the underlying or related instruments.

## Positive and negative GEX: common interpretations

- **Positive aggregate GEX:** hedging flows may counteract price moves and potentially dampen realised movement.
- **Negative aggregate GEX:** hedging flows may follow price moves and potentially amplify movement.

These are simplified interpretations, not laws. The effect depends on who holds the options, how they hedge, liquidity, other market participants, and the model's assumptions.

## Example using an index

If a model estimates positive GEX around a NIFTY strike near the current index level, an analyst might hypothesise that hedging could dampen moves around that region. It does not mean the strike is guaranteed support or resistance. News, institutional flows, and changing positions can overwhelm the estimated effect.

## How to use GEX responsibly

1. Treat GEX as context alongside price action, volume, open interest, and volatility.
2. Check the data timestamp and provider methodology.
3. Reassess after large price moves or option-chain changes.
4. Do not treat a “gamma flip” as a guaranteed turning point.
5. Never size a trade solely from a GEX reading.

## Limitations

- Public open interest alone does not reveal which side dealers hold.
- Providers can calculate different GEX values.
- Positions and hedges change quickly.
- A single model cannot capture every interaction between index options, futures, and cash-market flows.

## Key takeaway

GEX can help frame a hypothesis about options-related hedging flows, but it is not a standalone directional signal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does positive GEX mean the market must stay range-bound?
No. It is a model-based interpretation, not a promise about future prices.

### Is GEX the same as open interest?
No. Open interest counts outstanding contracts; GEX attempts to translate estimated Gamma and positioning assumptions into exposure.

### Can GEX predict the exact high or low?
No. It may provide context but cannot reliably identify exact turning points on its own.
