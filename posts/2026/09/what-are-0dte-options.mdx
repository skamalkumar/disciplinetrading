# What Are 0DTE Options?

**0DTE** means “zero days to expiration.” It refers to an option that expires on the current trading day. Availability, expiry calendars, contract specifications, and settlement rules differ by exchange and instrument. Always verify current NIFTY or SENSEX contract details instead of assuming every index has the same expiry schedule.

## Why 0DTE options behave differently

Very short-dated options have little time remaining. Near the money, Gamma can become large as expiry approaches, and extrinsic value can decay rapidly. Delta and premium may therefore change sharply after a relatively small move.

This creates opportunity and risk in both directions. A small favourable move may increase premium quickly, but an adverse move or a period of little movement can also cause rapid losses.

## A simplified example

A trader buys a near-the-money option with only hours remaining. If the index moves quickly in the expected direction, the premium may rise. If the index stalls, moves the other way, or IV falls, the premium may decline. There is no fixed return: outcomes depend on entry premium, strike, time, volatility, execution costs, and the underlying's movement.

## Common misconceptions

**“Cheap premium means low risk.”** A low premium can still lose 100% of the amount paid.

**“High Gamma means easy profit.”** Gamma increases sensitivity in favourable and unfavourable directions.

**“Expiry-day options suit everyone.”** They can require fast decisions and disciplined risk controls and may be unsuitable for inexperienced traders.

## Risk controls to consider

1. Define maximum loss before entry.
2. Account for bid-ask spread, fees, and slippage.
3. Avoid increasing size to recover a loss.
4. Do not assume a stop order guarantees the planned exit price in a fast market.
5. Understand settlement, expiry, and broker margin procedures.
6. Test a clearly defined rule set with transaction costs before considering live use.

## 0DTE vs longer-dated options

| Feature | 0DTE options | Longer-dated options |
|---|---|---|
| Time remaining | Very little | More time |
| Premium sensitivity near expiry | Can change rapidly | Depends on time and market conditions |
| Gamma near the money | Can be very high | Depends on time and other factors |
| Decision pressure | Often intense | May allow more time to adjust |
| Risk | Substantial | Also substantial, but the risk profile differs |

## Key takeaway

0DTE options are not simply cheaper versions of longer-dated options. Their risk profile is distinct, and rapid premium changes can work against the trader as quickly as they can work in their favour.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a 0DTE option expire worthless?
Yes. An option that finishes out of the money may expire worthless, subject to the applicable contract rules.

### Are 0DTE options always more volatile?
Their premiums and Greeks can be highly sensitive close to expiry, but underlying-market volatility varies by day and conditions.

### Should beginners trade 0DTE options?
They should understand payoff, Greeks, costs, and expiry rules first. This article is educational, not a recommendation to trade.
