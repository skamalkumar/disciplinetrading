# What Is Call Backspread?

A call backspread commonly sells one lower-strike Call and buys two higher-strike Calls. It is designed to benefit from a large upside move.

## How It Works

With NIFTY near 22,500, an illustrative structure is short one 22,600 Call and long two 22,800 Calls. Use actual premiums to calculate the net debit or credit and expiry payoff.

## Risk and Payoff

Below the short strike, the result depends on the net premium. Between the strikes, the short Call can create a loss zone. Above the long strikes, the two long Calls can make profits grow as the market rises. Calculate the maximum loss and all breakevens rather than assuming the trade is zero-cost.

## Practical Checklist

Before placing a trade, calculate the payoff at several expiry prices using live premiums. Include lot size, brokerage, taxes, bid-ask spread and slippage. Define the maximum acceptable loss and the conditions that would invalidate the trade. Do not rely on premium received as evidence that a trade is safe.

## Final Thoughts

A call backspread targets a large upside move. Strike selection, premium and the size of the expected move determine whether the structure is suitable.
