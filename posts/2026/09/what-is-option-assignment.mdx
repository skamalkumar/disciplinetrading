## What Is Option Assignment?

Option Assignment occurs when an option seller is required to fulfil the obligation created by the option contract.

In simple terms:

**Exercise is the action taken by the option holder. Assignment is the corresponding obligation placed on an option writer.**

The exact mechanics depend on the contract, exercise style, clearing process and current exchange rules.

For a foundation, see [What Are Options? A Beginner's Guide to Call and Put Options](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-are-options).

## Assignment vs Exercise

These terms are closely connected but are not the same.

**Exercise:** The option holder uses the right associated with the option.

**Assignment:** An option writer is selected to fulfil the corresponding obligation.

For a call option, the obligation can involve delivering or selling the underlying according to the contract terms.

For a put option, the obligation can involve buying the underlying according to the contract terms.

The exact settlement mechanism depends on the specific derivative contract.

## Why Does Assignment Matter?

An option seller may think an option is simply a premium-collection position.

But selling an option creates an obligation.

This is particularly important when positions approach expiry or when an option is in-the-money and applicable exercise or assignment procedures become relevant.

For expiry fundamentals, see [What Is Option Expiry? A Beginner's Guide](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-option-expiry).

## Can an Option Seller Be Assigned Before Expiry?

The answer depends on the exercise style and contract specifications.

American-style options can generally be exercised before expiry, creating potential early assignment for writers.

European-style options are generally exercised only according to the rules at expiry.

Indian exchange-traded contracts can have their own settlement and exercise framework, so traders should verify current exchange specifications rather than relying on a generic textbook assumption.

## Assignment Risk for Option Sellers

Assignment risk is one reason option sellers should understand the complete position.

Potential considerations include:

- Remaining time to expiry
- Whether the option is in-the-money
- Contract type
- Exercise style
- Dividend-related factors where applicable
- Settlement mechanism
- Margin requirements
- Position size

For option sellers, risk does not end simply because premium was collected.

## Assignment and Option Premium

The premium received by an option seller is compensation for taking on an obligation.

## Assignment and Margin

Option sellers may have margin obligations.

A position can require additional funds when the underlying moves significantly or when applicable risk parameters change.

For option pricing sensitivity, see [What Are Option Greeks? A Beginner's Guide](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-are-option-greeks).

## How Beginners Can Reduce Assignment Surprises

A disciplined checklist is:

1. Identify whether the option is European or American style.
2. Check the exact contract specifications.
3. Know the expiry date.
4. Monitor whether the option is ITM or OTM.
5. Understand the settlement method.
6. Check margin requirements.
7. Know what happens if the position remains open near expiry.
8. Manage the position according to a predefined risk plan.

## Common Mistakes

### Treating Premium as Free Income

Premium is received, but the seller takes on an obligation.

### Ignoring Contract Specifications

Different contracts can have different settlement and exercise rules.

### Holding Until Expiry Without a Plan

Expiry can create a very different risk profile from an earlier stage of the trade.

### Using Excessive Position Size

A relatively small premium does not mean the underlying risk is small.

## Final Thoughts

Option Assignment is an important concept for anyone selling options.

The key distinction is simple: the holder exercises a right, while the writer can be assigned the corresponding obligation.

Understanding assignment, settlement, expiry and margin requirements is essential before holding short-option positions close to expiry.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is option assignment?

It is the process through which an option writer is required to fulfil the obligation associated with an exercised option.

### Is assignment the same as exercise?

No. Exercise is the holder's action; assignment affects the writer.

### Can all options be assigned before expiry?

Not necessarily. The answer depends on the exercise style and contract specifications.

### Should option sellers understand assignment?

Yes. Anyone selling options should understand the applicable exercise, assignment, settlement and margin rules.
