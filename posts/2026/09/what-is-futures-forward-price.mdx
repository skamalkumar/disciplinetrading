## What Is the Forward or Fair Futures Price?

The **forward price** is the theoretical price agreed today for a transaction at a future date.

For exchange-traded futures, traders often estimate a **fair futures price** from the spot price and the economics of carrying the position to expiry.

For an equity index such as NIFTY, financing costs and expected dividends are important.

## Simple Formula

A simplified relationship is:

**Fair Futures Value ≈ Spot Price + Cost of Carry − Expected Income**

A continuous-compounding version is:

**F = S × e^((r − q)T)**

Where:

- F = theoretical futures price
- S = spot price
- r = financing rate
- q = dividend yield
- T = time to expiry

Actual market prices can differ because of supply, demand, transaction costs and market conditions.

## NIFTY Example

Suppose:

- NIFTY spot = 25,000
- Financing rate = 6%
- Expected dividend yield = 1%
- Time to expiry = 0.25 years

Then:

**F ≈ 25,000 × e^((0.06 − 0.01) × 0.25)**

The theoretical value is slightly above spot.

The actual NIFTY futures price may trade above or below that estimate.

## Why Can Futures Trade Above Spot?

When financing costs exceed the economic benefit of expected dividends, futures can trade at a premium to spot.

This difference is commonly analysed through **basis**.

## Why Can Futures Trade Below Spot?

If expected dividends and other carry benefits outweigh financing costs, futures can trade below spot.

Supply and demand can also influence the observed price.

## Futures and Expiry

As expiry approaches, futures and spot generally converge according to the contract's settlement mechanism.

This convergence is fundamental to futures pricing.

## Futures vs Forward

A forward is generally an over-the-counter agreement.

A futures contract is standardized and exchange traded.

They can differ in:

- Standardization
- Margining
- Settlement
- Counterparty structure
- Liquidity

## Practical NIFTY Example

Suppose:

- NIFTY spot = 25,000
- NIFTY futures = 25,080

Basis = 25,080 − 25,000 = **+80 points**

The 80-point premium does not automatically mean futures are overpriced. It should be compared with theoretical carry.

## Why Fair Value Matters

Understanding fair value helps with:

- Futures trading
- Hedging
- Cash-and-carry concepts
- Arbitrage analysis
- Understanding futures premiums and discounts

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is fair value the same as market futures price?

No. Fair value is a theoretical estimate; the market price reflects actual trading.

### Why are NIFTY futures sometimes above spot?

Financing costs, expected dividends and market conditions can create a premium.

### Do futures converge with spot?

Near expiry, futures and spot generally converge according to the applicable settlement mechanism.

## Final Thoughts

Futures pricing is fundamentally about **spot price plus or minus the economics of carrying the position to expiry**.

Understanding financing, dividends, fair value and basis makes NIFTY futures pricing much easier to interpret.
