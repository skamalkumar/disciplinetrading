# What Is a Share Buyback?

A **share buyback** occurs when a company repurchases its own shares through a permitted method. Depending on the structure and rules, repurchased shares may be extinguished, reducing the number of shares outstanding.

## Why do companies buy back shares?

- Return surplus capital to shareholders.
- Change the capital structure.
- Offset dilution from employee share plans in some cases.
- Signal management's view that shares may be undervalued.

An announcement alone does not prove that the stock is undervalued. The price paid and the company's motives matter.

## How a buyback can affect EPS

Suppose a company earns ₹100 crore and has 10 crore shares outstanding. Simplified EPS is ₹10. If shares outstanding later fall to 9 crore while earnings remain ₹100 crore, simplified EPS becomes about ₹11.11. This mechanical increase does not mean operating profit grew. The company has also used cash that could otherwise fund investment, debt repayment, or dividends.

## Potential benefits and risks

**Potential benefits:** returns capital, may improve per-share metrics, and can create value when shares are repurchased below intrinsic value.

**Potential risks:** the company may overpay, cash reserves may fall, debt-funded buybacks can increase leverage, and EPS may rise mechanically despite flat or weakening operations.

## Investor checklist

1. Compare the buyback price with a reasonable estimate of intrinsic value.
2. Review free cash flow, cash balances, and debt.
3. Understand the method, size, timeline, and eligibility terms.
4. Consider whether the company has attractive reinvestment opportunities.
5. Separate EPS changes caused by share count from those caused by operating earnings.

## Key takeaway

A buyback can create value when executed sensibly, but it is not automatically bullish. Price paid, funding source, and business outlook matter.

## FAQs

### Does a buyback always increase the share price?
No. Price depends on performance, valuation, market conditions, and how investors interpret the transaction.

### Does a buyback always improve EPS?
A lower share count can mechanically increase EPS if earnings are unchanged. Falling earnings or financing costs can alter the result.

### Is a buyback better than a dividend?
Neither is always better. The choice depends on valuation, shareholder circumstances, taxes, regulation, and capital needs.
