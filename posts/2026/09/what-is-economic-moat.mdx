# What Is an Economic Moat?

An **economic moat** is a durable competitive advantage that helps a company defend its profitability against competitors. The idea is to identify businesses that can protect returns and cash generation over time—not merely companies that are popular or growing quickly.

## Common sources of a moat

- **Brand strength:** customers trust or prefer the product and may accept a premium price.
- **Switching costs:** moving to a competing product takes time, money, or operational effort.
- **Network effects:** a service becomes more useful as more people or businesses use it.
- **Cost advantage:** the company can produce or distribute at a structurally lower cost.
- **Intangible assets:** patents, licences, proprietary technology, or specialised know-how provide protection where legally and commercially sustainable.
- **Scale advantages:** size, distribution, or infrastructure makes it difficult for smaller competitors to match the economics.

## How to evaluate a possible moat

Look for evidence across several years, not just a single strong quarter:

1. Stable or improving margins despite competition.
2. Consistent returns on capital and cash generation.
3. Customer retention, pricing power, or market-share evidence where disclosed.
4. A credible explanation of why competitors cannot easily replicate the advantage.
5. Capital allocation that supports rather than erodes the advantage.

## Moat does not mean any price is fair

A high-quality company can still be a poor investment if bought at an excessive valuation. Compare the price with reasonable expectations for growth, margins, reinvestment, and risk. A moat can also weaken because of technology shifts, regulation, new competitors, or poor management decisions.

## Key takeaway

A moat is a hypothesis to test with evidence. Assess the durability of the advantage, its effect on returns and cash flow, and the price you are paying.

## FAQs

### Is a famous brand always an economic moat?
No. Brand awareness only helps if it supports customer preference, pricing power, retention, or other measurable economics.

### Can a moat disappear?
Yes. Technology, regulation, customer preferences, competition, and execution can erode an advantage.

### Does a strong moat guarantee investment returns?
No. Returns also depend on valuation, business performance, capital allocation, and broader risks.
