# What Is Operating Leverage?

**Operating leverage** measures how sensitive a company's operating profit is to changes in revenue.

A company with high fixed operating costs can see EBIT rise rapidly when revenue increases, but EBIT can also fall rapidly when revenue declines.

## Formula

**Degree of Operating Leverage (DOL) = % Change in EBIT ÷ % Change in Revenue**

A common approximation is:

**DOL = Contribution Margin ÷ EBIT**

## Simple Example

Suppose:

- Revenue = ₹1,000 crore
- Variable costs = ₹600 crore
- Fixed operating costs = ₹250 crore

Contribution = ₹400 crore.

EBIT = ₹150 crore.

Therefore:

**DOL = ₹400 ÷ ₹150 = 2.67**

Around that operating level, a 1% change in revenue could produce roughly a 2.67% change in EBIT, assuming the cost structure remains broadly stable.

## NIFTY-Style Example

| Metric | Company A | Company B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | ₹10,000 Cr | ₹10,000 Cr |
| Variable costs | ₹7,000 Cr | ₹5,000 Cr |
| Fixed operating costs | ₹2,000 Cr | ₹4,000 Cr |
| EBIT | ₹1,000 Cr | ₹1,000 Cr |

Company B has the heavier fixed-cost structure. If revenue accelerates, EBIT can grow faster; if revenue falls, EBIT can also fall faster.

## Operating vs Financial Leverage

| Operating Leverage | Financial Leverage |
|---|---|
| Driven mainly by fixed operating costs | Driven mainly by debt |
| Affects EBIT sensitivity | Affects earnings after interest |
| Business-model related | Capital-structure related |

## What Investors Should Examine

Look at:

- revenue growth,
- gross margin,
- fixed operating expenses,
- EBIT growth,
- capacity utilisation,
- pricing power, and
- performance during weak demand.

Operating leverage is not constant because costs can change over time.

## Final Thoughts

The key question is not merely whether revenue is growing, but:

**How much of that revenue growth is flowing through to operating profit?**
