# What Is Put Backspread?

A put backspread usually sells one higher-strike Put and buys two lower-strike Puts. It is designed to benefit from a sharp decline, but may have a loss zone between the strikes.

## How It Works

Sell 1 NIFTY 22,400 Put and buy 2 NIFTY 22,200 Puts as an illustration. Calculate the net premium, maximum loss and breakevens from actual premiums before entry. A moderate decline can still leave the trade in its loss zone.

## Risk and Payoff

The payoff depends on strike spacing and premium. Test several expiry prices, including a sharp fall, a moderate fall and a close above the short strike. Consider liquidity, volatility, slippage and a pre-defined loss limit.

## Practical Checklist

Before placing a trade, calculate the payoff at several expiry prices using live premiums. Include lot size, brokerage, taxes, bid-ask spread and slippage. Define the maximum acceptable loss and the conditions that would invalidate the trade. Do not rely on premium received as evidence that a trade is safe.

## Final Thoughts

A put backspread is a downside-volatility structure, not simply a bearish trade. It is most useful when the expected move is large enough to overcome the loss zone and costs.
