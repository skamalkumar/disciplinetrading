# What Is a Stock Split?

A **stock split** increases the number of shares by dividing each existing share into a specified number of shares. The per-share price generally adjusts proportionately, all else equal, so the split itself does not create extra economic value.

## Example

Suppose you own 10 shares priced at ₹1,000 each, worth ₹10,000 in total. In a 1-for-5 split, you would hold 50 shares at a theoretical price of about ₹200 each. The holding's theoretical value remains ₹10,000, before market movements and other factors.

| Measure | Before | After 1-for-5 split |
|---|---:|---:|
| Shares held | 10 | 50 |
| Illustrative price | ₹1,000 | ₹200 |
| Illustrative value | ₹10,000 | ₹10,000 |

## Why companies split shares

- Reduce the nominal price per share.
- Make share quantities more accessible to some investors.
- Improve trading convenience.
- Adjust the share structure without changing proportional ownership by itself.

A lower share price does not automatically mean a stock is cheaper on a valuation basis.

## Stock split vs bonus issue

A split subdivides existing shares. A bonus issue gives eligible shareholders additional shares by capitalising eligible reserves, subject to applicable rules. Neither action alone guarantees a gain.

## Investor checklist

1. Read the company's official exchange filing for the ratio and record date.
2. Check that share counts and historical price series are adjusted correctly.
3. Assess valuation, earnings, cash flow, debt, and business quality independently.
4. Avoid buying solely because the post-split price looks lower.

## Key takeaway

A stock split changes the unit count and price per share, not the underlying business value by itself.

## FAQs

### Does a stock split increase investment value?
Not by itself. Share count rises while the theoretical per-share price adjusts. Market movements can change value afterward.

### Is a ₹100 stock cheaper than a ₹1,000 stock?
Not necessarily. Compare valuation, earnings, cash flow, growth, and share count rather than nominal prices alone.

### What is a record date?
It is the date used to determine eligible shareholders for a corporate action, subject to applicable settlement and exchange rules.
