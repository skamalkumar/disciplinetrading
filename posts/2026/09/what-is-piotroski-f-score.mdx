The Piotroski F-Score is a nine-signal fundamental-analysis framework used to assess financial strength and improvement. The standard framework scores profitability, leverage/liquidity/funding, and operating-efficiency signals from 0 or 1, producing a total from 0 to 9.

### The Nine Signals
The profitability group considers positive ROA, positive operating cash flow, improving ROA, and operating cash flow exceeding net income. The leverage/liquidity group considers improving leverage, improving liquidity, and the absence of new equity issuance. The efficiency group considers improving gross margin and improving asset turnover.

### Why It Matters
The score can help investors distinguish financially stronger and weaker companies, particularly within a value-oriented universe. A high score does not mean a stock is cheap, and a low score does not automatically mean a business is poor.

### How Investors Can Use It
1. Gather consistent financial statements.
2. Calculate the nine signals.
3. Score each signal.
4. Add the points.
5. Compare the score historically and with peers.
6. Investigate which signals changed.
7. Review valuation, cash flow, debt, growth and business quality separately.

### Common Mistakes
Do not treat F-Score as a buy signal, use inconsistent accounting data, ignore industry context, or look only at the total score without examining the individual signals.

## Final Thoughts
Piotroski F-Score provides a disciplined snapshot of financial improvement. It works best alongside valuation, profitability, cash flow, leverage and business analysis.
