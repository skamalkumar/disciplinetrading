# What Is Naked Put?

A naked Put is a short Put without a fully protective offsetting position. The seller receives premium but takes downside risk if the underlying falls.

## How It Works

Assume NIFTY is 22,500 and a trader sells a 22,300 Put for 70 points. Expiry breakeven is 22,230. If NIFTY expires at 22,000, intrinsic value is 300 and loss before costs is 230 points.

## Risk and Payoff

Maximum profit is the premium received. Loss can be substantial after a sharp fall. Index-option settlement differs from physical delivery of shares, so check the contract specifications. Consider gaps, implied volatility, margin increases, liquidity and slippage. A bull Put spread adds a lower-strike long Put to cap expiry risk based on the spread width and net credit.

## Practical Checklist

Before placing a trade, calculate the payoff at several expiry prices using live premiums. Include lot size, brokerage, taxes, bid-ask spread and slippage. Define the maximum acceptable loss and the conditions that would invalidate the trade. Do not rely on premium received as evidence that a trade is safe.

## Final Thoughts

A naked Put offers limited premium income with meaningful downside exposure. Calculate the risk and plan exits before entry.
