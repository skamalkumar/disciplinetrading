## What Is EV/EBITDA?

EV/EBITDA is a valuation multiple that compares a company's Enterprise Value with its EBITDA.

In simple terms:

**How much enterprise value is the market assigning to each unit of EBITDA?**

Start with [What Is Enterprise Value? A Beginner's Guide](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-enterprise-value) and [What Is EBITDA? A Beginner's Guide](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-ebitda).

## EV/EBITDA Formula

**EV/EBITDA = Enterprise Value ÷ EBITDA**

Suppose Enterprise Value is ₹20,000 crore and EBITDA is ₹2,000 crore.

**EV/EBITDA = 20,000 ÷ 2,000 = 10×**

## Why Do Investors Use EV/EBITDA?

P/E compares equity value with net earnings.

EV/EBITDA uses Enterprise Value and EBITDA, making it useful when comparing businesses with different debt and cash positions.

For comparison, see [What Is P/E Ratio? A Beginner's Guide](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-pe-ratio).

## What Does a Low EV/EBITDA Mean?

A lower multiple means enterprise value is lower relative to EBITDA.

It may indicate a lower valuation relative to current operating earnings, but it is not automatically a bargain.

A low multiple can reflect:

- Weak growth expectations
- Cyclical earnings
- High business risk
- Heavy capital expenditure
- Weak competitive position
- Temporary peak EBITDA

## What Does a High EV/EBITDA Mean?

A high multiple means investors are paying more relative to current EBITDA.

It may reflect strong growth expectations, recurring earnings, competitive advantages or expectations of future margin expansion.

But higher expectations also create valuation risk if growth disappoints.

## A Simple Comparison

**Company A**
- EV: ₹10,000 crore
- EBITDA: ₹1,000 crore
- EV/EBITDA: 10×

**Company B**
- EV: ₹15,000 crore
- EBITDA: ₹1,000 crore
- EV/EBITDA: 15×

If the businesses are comparable, Company B carries the higher valuation multiple.

That alone does not make Company A the better investment.

## EV/EBITDA vs P/E

| Measure | Numerator | Denominator |
|---|---|---|
| P/E | Equity market value | Net earnings |
| EV/EBITDA | Enterprise Value | EBITDA |

They provide different perspectives and can be used together.

## EV/EBITDA and Debt

Debt affects Enterprise Value, while EBITDA is measured before interest.

This makes the multiple useful for some capital-structure comparisons.

However, EBITDA is not cash available to repay debt.

See [What Is Debt-to-Equity Ratio? A Beginner's Guide](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-debt-to-equity-ratio).

## EV/EBITDA and Cash Flow

A company can have high EBITDA but substantial capital expenditure and working-capital requirements.

Therefore, also review [What Is Free Cash Flow? A Beginner's Guide](https://disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-free-cash-flow).

## Industry Comparison

EV/EBITDA varies significantly across industries.

The most useful comparisons are usually:

- The same company over time
- Direct competitors
- Similar business models
- Companies with comparable growth and profitability

## How Investors Can Use EV/EBITDA

1. Understand the business.
2. Verify Enterprise Value.
3. Review EBITDA quality.
4. Calculate EV/EBITDA.
5. Compare historical valuation.
6. Compare relevant peers.
7. Review growth and margins.
8. Examine debt and capital expenditure.
9. Review cash flow.
10. Assess whether the valuation is justified.

## Common Mistakes

### Looking Only at the Multiple

Business quality matters.

### Ignoring EBITDA Quality

Temporary earnings can make a multiple appear artificially low.

### Ignoring Capital Expenditure

EBITDA does not deduct capital expenditure.

### Comparing Different Industries

Normal valuation ranges can vary substantially.

## Final Thoughts

EV/EBITDA connects Enterprise Value with operating earnings.

Its greatest usefulness comes from comparison rather than from one isolated number.

Use it alongside P/E, growth, margins, debt, cash flow and business quality.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does EV/EBITDA mean?

It means Enterprise Value divided by EBITDA.

### Is a lower EV/EBITDA always better?

No. A low multiple can reflect weak growth or higher risk.

### Why use EV instead of market capitalization?

EV incorporates debt and cash in the simplified framework.

### Is EBITDA cash flow?

No. EBITDA does not account for capital expenditure and working-capital requirements.
