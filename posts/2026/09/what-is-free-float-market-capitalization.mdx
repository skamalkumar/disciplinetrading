# What Is Free-Float Market Capitalization?

**Free-float market capitalization** estimates the market value of shares considered available for public trading, rather than multiplying the share price by every share issued. The exact free-float definition and exclusions depend on the exchange or index methodology.

## Formula

**Free-float market cap = Share price × shares considered available for public trading**

For example, suppose a company has 10 crore shares outstanding at ₹200 each. Its total market capitalization is ₹2,000 crore. If the applicable methodology treats 40% of shares as free float, the illustrative free-float market cap is ₹800 crore.

## Why the distinction matters

Promoter holdings, strategic holdings, and other restricted or non-free-float holdings may not be treated as freely available to trade under a particular methodology. Index providers may use free-float market capitalization when determining index weights, so two companies with similar total market caps can have different index weights.

## What free float does not tell you

Free float is not the same as daily trading volume, liquidity, or valuation. A stock with a large free float can still have weak liquidity, while a smaller free float can sometimes trade actively. Review bid-ask spreads, traded value, ownership concentration, and the methodology used.

## Investor checklist

1. Confirm total shares outstanding and the latest shareholding pattern.
2. Check the exchange or index provider's free-float methodology.
3. Distinguish total market cap from free-float market cap in comparisons.
4. Assess liquidity using traded value and bid-ask spreads, not free float alone.

## Key takeaway

Total market cap measures the value of all outstanding equity at the market price; free-float market cap focuses on the portion considered available to public investors under a stated methodology.

## FAQs

### Is free-float market cap always lower than total market cap?
Usually it is no greater than total market cap when the free-float portion is a subset of outstanding shares, though the precise calculation depends on the methodology and data.

### Does a high free float guarantee liquidity?
No. Liquidity also depends on trading activity, market makers, spreads, investor participation, and market conditions.

### Why do indices use free float?
It can make index weights more reflective of the shares available for public investment, according to the index provider's rules.
