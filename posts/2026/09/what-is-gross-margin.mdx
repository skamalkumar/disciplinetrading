## What Is Gross Margin?

**Gross Margin** measures the percentage of revenue remaining after direct costs associated with producing goods or delivering services.

It helps investors understand the economics of a company's core offering before operating expenses.

## Formula

**Gross Margin = (Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold) ÷ Revenue × 100**

And:

**Gross Profit = Revenue − COGS**

## Example

Suppose:

- Revenue = ₹1,000 crore
- COGS = ₹600 crore

Gross Profit = ₹400 crore.

Gross Margin = ₹400 ÷ ₹1,000 × 100 = **40%**

The company generates ₹40 of gross profit for every ₹100 of revenue.

## What Does a High Gross Margin Mean?

A high margin can indicate:

- Pricing power
- Efficient production
- Premium products
- Attractive product economics

But a high margin is not automatically evidence of a superior investment.

## Gross Margin Trend

| Year | Gross Margin |
|---|---:|
| FY2024 | 36% |
| FY2025 | 39% |
| FY2026 | 42% |

An improving margin may result from better pricing, lower input costs, improved product mix or operating efficiency.

The investor should identify the actual reason.

## Gross Margin and Pricing Power

If input costs rise and a company can increase prices without losing substantial demand, Gross Margin may remain stable.

If the company cannot pass costs to customers, the margin can fall.

## Gross Margin vs Operating Margin

Gross Margin accounts for direct costs.

Operating Margin goes further and accounts for operating expenses such as employees, marketing, administration and research.

For example:

Revenue = ₹1,000 crore

Gross Profit = ₹400 crore

Operating expenses = ₹250 crore

Operating Profit = ₹150 crore

Gross Margin = **40%**

Operating Margin = **15%**

Company A has stronger gross economics on these figures, but valuation and future growth still matter.

## Advantages

- Shows direct cost economics
- Useful for pricing-power analysis
- Good for peer comparison
- Useful for trend analysis

## Limitations

- Accounting classifications can differ
- Industry margins vary greatly
- High Gross Margin does not guarantee high net profit
- One-year changes can be temporary

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is higher Gross Margin always better?

No. Compare it with industry peers, company history and the business model.

### What can cause Gross Margin to fall?

Higher input costs, lower selling prices and adverse product mix are common causes.

## Final Thoughts

Gross Margin is a useful way to understand **how much value remains after direct costs**.

Track the trend rather than relying on one year's number.
