## What Is Probability of Profit?

Probability of Profit (POP) is an estimate of the likelihood that an options position will finish with a positive profit under a defined set of assumptions. It is a probability estimate, not a promise and not a measure of how much money a trade may make.

A platform may estimate POP using an option-pricing model, implied volatility, the underlying price, time to expiry and the strategy's breakeven points. Different platforms can use different assumptions and calculation methods.

## A Simple Example

Suppose a hypothetical NIFTY credit spread has a maximum possible profit of ₹2,000 and a maximum possible loss of ₹8,000. A model estimates a 70% probability of finishing profitable.

That 70% estimate does not mean the trader will make ₹2,000 seven times out of every ten trades. Outcomes are not guaranteed to follow a neat sequence, and the loss on an unsuccessful trade may be much larger than the gain on a successful one.

## POP Is Not the Same as Expected Return

Consider two simplified strategies:

| Measure | Strategy A | Strategy B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Estimated POP | 80% | 55% |
| Profit if successful | ₹500 | ₹1,500 |
| Loss if unsuccessful | ₹2,500 | ₹700 |

A higher POP does not automatically make Strategy A superior. The size of gains and losses, transaction costs and the reliability of the probability estimate all matter.

## What Influences an Options POP Estimate?

- **Implied volatility:** A model's expected price distribution is strongly influenced by the volatility input.
- **Time to expiry:** More time generally allows a wider range of possible underlying prices.
- **Breakeven levels:** The strategy's payoff structure determines which terminal prices produce a profit.
- **Underlying price:** The distance between spot and breakeven levels matters.
- **Model assumptions:** Distribution assumptions, volatility dynamics and interest rates can affect the estimate.

## Important Limitations

POP estimates commonly simplify real markets. Actual returns can be affected by changing implied volatility, volatility skew, gaps, early exits, bid-ask spreads, brokerage, taxes, liquidity and execution quality. A probability calculated at entry may not remain valid after the market moves.

For multi-leg positions, a probability estimate based only on expiry payoff may also overlook the difficulty or cost of managing the position before expiry.

## How to Use POP Responsibly

1. Identify the exact strategy and expiry used in the calculation.
2. Confirm the breakeven prices and maximum gain and loss.
3. Compare POP with the potential reward-to-risk relationship.
4. Include realistic transaction costs and slippage.
5. Stress-test outcomes beyond the model's central range.
6. Decide position size from the loss you can tolerate, not from POP alone.

## POP and NIFTY or SENSEX Options

For index options, traders should check the correct contract multiplier, expiry and settlement specifications. A POP estimate for a NIFTY spread should not be transferred directly to a SENSEX spread or to a different expiry. Index gaps and changes in volatility can make realised outcomes differ materially from model estimates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is 80% POP a good trade?

Not necessarily. The potential loss, expected value, costs, liquidity and model assumptions must also be considered.

### Does POP mean the chance of touching profit?

Not always. Many platforms report the probability of finishing profitable at expiry. A probability of touching a price level is a different measure.

### Can POP change after I enter a trade?

Yes. Changes in the underlying price, implied volatility and time remaining can change the estimate.

### Is POP accurate?

It is an estimate based on a model and its inputs. It should not be treated as a forecast or certainty.

## Final Thoughts

Probability of Profit can help compare possible outcomes, but it is only one part of options analysis. A disciplined trader considers POP alongside payoff, expected value, liquidity, costs and risk limits.
