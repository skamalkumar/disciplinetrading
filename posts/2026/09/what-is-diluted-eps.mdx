# What Is Diluted EPS?

**Diluted Earnings Per Share (Diluted EPS)** shows earnings per share after considering potentially dilutive securities that could become ordinary shares.

Examples include employee stock options, warrants and convertible securities.

The key question is:

> What could earnings per share look like if potentially dilutive securities became shares?

## Basic EPS vs Diluted EPS

A simplified Basic EPS formula is:

**Basic EPS = Profit attributable to ordinary shareholders ÷ Weighted average ordinary shares**

Diluted EPS adjusts for potentially dilutive ordinary shares and, where applicable, related earnings adjustments.

In simple terms:

**Diluted EPS = Adjusted earnings ÷ Adjusted weighted average shares**

## Simple Example

Suppose:

- Profit = ₹1,000 crore
- Weighted average shares = 100 crore

Basic EPS:

₹1,000 ÷ 100 = **₹10**

Now suppose potentially dilutive securities add 5 crore shares under the applicable calculation.

Simplified diluted EPS:

₹1,000 ÷ 105 = **₹9.52**

The potential additional shares reduce EPS.

## Why Does Dilution Matter?

If the number of shares increases without a proportional increase in earnings, each share can represent a smaller portion of the company's earnings.

| Metric | Basic | Diluted |
|---|---:|---:|
| Profit | ₹1,000 cr | ₹1,000 cr |
| Shares | 100 cr | 105 cr |
| EPS | ₹10.00 | ₹9.52 |

## What Can Cause Dilution?

Potential dilution can come from:

- Employee stock options
- Convertible bonds
- Convertible preference shares
- Warrants
- Other potentially dilutive instruments

The exact accounting treatment depends on the instrument and applicable reporting rules.

## Diluted EPS Is Not Always Lower

Potential securities are included when they are dilutive.

If including a potential security would increase EPS, it is generally considered anti-dilutive and is not included in diluted EPS.

Therefore, diluted EPS can be equal to basic EPS when there is no dilution.

## Diluted EPS and P/E Ratio

A simplified P/E formula is:

**P/E = Share Price ÷ EPS**

Suppose a stock trades at ₹500.

Using basic EPS of ₹10:

P/E = **50x**

Using diluted EPS of ₹9.52:

P/E ≈ **52.5x**

The diluted figure can therefore give a more conservative earnings-based valuation.

## Example: NIFTY Company

Imagine a hypothetical NIFTY company reports:

- Profit = ₹2,400 crore
- Basic weighted average shares = 120 crore
- Potential dilutive shares = 8 crore

Basic EPS = ₹2,400 ÷ 120 = **₹20**

Simplified diluted EPS = ₹2,400 ÷ 128 = **₹18.75**

An investor should understand which EPS figure is being used for valuation.

## What Investors Should Check

When reading annual reports, look for:

1. Basic EPS
2. Diluted EPS
3. Weighted average shares
4. Employee stock options
5. Convertible securities
6. Changes in share count

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is diluted EPS better than basic EPS?

Neither is universally better. Basic EPS reflects actual weighted-average ordinary shares, while diluted EPS considers potential dilutive shares.

### Can diluted EPS equal basic EPS?

Yes, when there are no dilutive potential ordinary shares.

### Does dilution mean the company is bad?

No. Employee equity or convertible securities can be legitimate parts of compensation or financing. The important issue is whether dilution is reasonable relative to growth and value creation.

### Which EPS should I use for valuation?

Understand both. Diluted EPS is particularly useful when potential dilution is economically meaningful.

## Final Thoughts

Diluted EPS helps investors understand how potential share issuance can affect earnings attributable to each share.

A company can increase total profit while EPS grows much more slowly if its share count expands significantly.
