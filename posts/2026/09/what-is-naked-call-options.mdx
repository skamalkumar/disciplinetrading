# What Is Naked Call?

A naked Call is a short Call that is not covered by owning the underlying or by another position that limits risk. The seller receives premium but accepts potentially very large losses if the market rises.

## How It Works

Assume NIFTY is 22,500 and a trader sells a 22,700 Call for 80 points. Expiry breakeven is 22,780. If NIFTY expires at 22,900, the Call's intrinsic value is 200 and the loss before costs is 120 points.

## Risk and Payoff

Maximum profit is the premium received; the upside loss is theoretically unlimited. Margin is collateral, not a maximum-loss limit. Gaps, rising volatility, gamma near expiry, slippage and margin changes can make losses escalate quickly. Compare the risk with a defined-risk bear Call spread.

## Practical Checklist

Before placing a trade, calculate the payoff at several expiry prices using live premiums. Include lot size, brokerage, taxes, bid-ask spread and slippage. Define the maximum acceptable loss and the conditions that would invalidate the trade. Do not rely on premium received as evidence that a trade is safe.

## Final Thoughts

A naked Call is not a low-risk income strategy: premium is capped while losses can keep growing. Understand the payoff and maximum acceptable risk before trading.
