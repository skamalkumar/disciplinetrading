# What Is Long Call Strategy?

A long Call is the purchase of a Call option for bullish exposure with a maximum loss generally limited to the premium paid.

## How It Works

Suppose NIFTY is 22,500 and a trader buys a 22,600 Call for 100 points. At expiry at 22,900, intrinsic value is 300 points and profit before costs is 200 points. At or below 22,600, the option expires without intrinsic value and the 100-point premium is lost.

## Risk and Payoff

Maximum loss is the premium paid. Expiry breakeven is strike plus premium: 22,700 in this example. The Call can lose value if the move is too small or too slow, time passes, or implied volatility falls. Monitor Delta, Gamma, Theta and Vega.

## Practical Checklist

Before placing a trade, calculate the payoff at several expiry prices using live premiums. Include lot size, brokerage, taxes, bid-ask spread and slippage. Define the maximum acceptable loss and the conditions that would invalidate the trade. Do not rely on premium received as evidence that a trade is safe.

## Final Thoughts

A long Call offers leveraged upside with defined premium risk, but the market must move far enough and soon enough. Define the time horizon and maximum rupee loss before entry.
