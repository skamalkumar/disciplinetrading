## Open Interest (OI) Analysis

Total Call OI stands at 47.83 lakh contracts against Total Put OI of 51.13 lakh contracts, a fairly balanced split with Puts marginally ahead for this expiry. If you're not familiar with how Open Interest is built up and interpreted, [What Is Open Interest? A Beginner's Guide to OI in Options Trading](https://www.disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-open-interest) covers the basics.

The OI distribution across strikes shows a distinct pattern this week:

- The heaviest OI on the entire chain, for both Calls and Puts, sits at the 73,500 strike, just above current spot. This pivot zone stands out well above every other strike on the board.
- A secondary Call OI concentration appears at 74,000, marking the next resistance zone above the pivot.
- The heaviest Put OI below spot is at the 73,000 strike, marking the primary support zone, with a further Put cluster at 72,500 and another at 74,000 alongside the Call OI there.

## Change in OI Analysis

Looking at fresh Open Interest additions for the day rather than the cumulative total gives a read on where positioning is actively building right now.

Fresh writing is heaviest at the 73,500 strike on both sides, with Call additions slightly ahead of Put additions, reinforcing it as the key zone to watch this week. Further fresh Put additions appear at 73,000, 72,500 and 73,200, while the next notable Call addition is at 74,000.

This lines up with the cumulative OI picture: option writers are converging on 73,500 as the pivotal level for the week, with Put-side support building at 73,000 beneath it and Call-side resistance building at 74,000 above it.

## Put-Call Ratio (PCR) Analysis

The current PCR for this expiry is 1.06, meaning Put OI and Call OI are close to evenly matched, with Puts marginally ahead.

A PCR sitting close to 1 generally reflects a fairly balanced options market, without a strong directional lean from option writers on either side. This is a more neutral reading than a PCR skewed well above or below 1, and marks a clear cooling from the 1.60 reading on the previous SENSEX expiry.

That said, PCR is one data point among several, and a single week's reading should be considered alongside price action and the broader OI structure rather than in isolation.
