## Open Interest (OI) Analysis

Total Call OI stands at 37.96 lakh contracts against Total Put OI of 44.66 lakh contracts, with Put OI running ahead of Call OI for this expiry, a swing from the Call-heavy skew of the previous SENSEX expiry. If you're not familiar with how Open Interest is built up and interpreted, [What Is Open Interest? A Beginner's Guide to OI in Options Trading](https://www.disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-open-interest) covers the basics.

The OI distribution across strikes shows a distinct pattern this week:

- The heaviest Call OI on the entire chain sits at the 83,000 strike, right at current spot, with a heavy Put build-up alongside it, making it the key pivot zone for the week.
- The heaviest Put OI is concentrated at the 82,500 strike, below spot, with a near-equal Call build-up there too, marking a second pivot zone and the primary support area. A further Put cluster sits at 82,000.
- Above the pivot, Call OI thins out, with the next concentrations at 84,000 and 83,500, forming the resistance zones.

## Change in OI Analysis

Looking at fresh Open Interest additions for the day rather than the cumulative total gives a read on where positioning is actively building right now.

Fresh writing is heaviest at the 82,500 and 83,000 strikes, with Put additions clearly ahead of Call additions at both, reinforcing them as the key zones to watch this week. Further fresh Call additions appear at 83,500 and 84,000, while smaller Put additions appear at 82,000 and 82,800. Some Call unwinding is visible at 82,000.

This lines up with the cumulative OI picture: option writers are converging on 82,500 and 83,000 as the pivotal levels for the week, with Put-side conviction building faster than Call-side conviction around both.

## Put-Call Ratio (PCR) Analysis

The current PCR for this expiry is 1.17, meaning Put OI modestly outweighs Call OI.

A PCR above 1 generally reflects heavier Put writing relative to Call writing. Since option writers typically sell options they expect to expire worthless, a PCR in this range is conventionally read as a mildly bullish-to-neutral signal, with Put sellers showing a modest lean toward SENSEX holding above their strikes. This is a clear swing from the Call-heavy 0.85 reading on the previous SENSEX expiry.

That said, PCR is one data point among several, and a single week's reading should be considered alongside price action and the broader OI structure rather than in isolation.
