## Open Interest (OI) Analysis

Total Call OI stands at 34.59 lakh contracts against Total Put OI of 61.48 lakh contracts, with Put OI running well ahead of Call OI for this expiry, a much wider Put-side skew than last week. If you're not familiar with how Open Interest is built up and interpreted, [What Is Open Interest? A Beginner's Guide to OI in Options Trading](https://www.disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-open-interest) covers the basics.

The OI distribution across strikes shows clear concentration zones on both sides:

- The heaviest Put OI on the entire chain sits at the 74,000 strike, just below current spot, making it the primary support zone for the week. A secondary Put concentration appears at 73,500, with a further cluster at 74,100 right near spot.
- The 74,000 strike also carries the heaviest Call OI on the chain, so it stands out as the key pivot zone, with the Put side clearly ahead of the Call side there.
- Above spot, Call OI is lighter and more spread out, with the largest concentration at 75,000 and a further cluster at 74,500, marking the resistance zones for the week.

## Change in OI Analysis

Looking at fresh Open Interest additions for the day rather than the cumulative total gives a read on where positioning is actively building right now.

Fresh Put writing is heaviest at the 74,000 strike, reinforcing it as the key support level for the week, with further notable additions at 73,500 and 74,100. Fresh Call writing is also strongest at 74,000, though well behind Put additions there, with smaller additions at 74,100, 75,000 and 74,500.

This lines up with the cumulative OI picture: option writers are converging on 74,000 as the pivotal level for the week, with Put-side conviction clearly outpacing Call-side conviction around it and Call writing above spot staying comparatively light.

## Put-Call Ratio (PCR) Analysis

The current PCR for this expiry is 1.77, meaning Put OI outweighs Call OI by a wide margin.

A PCR well above 1 generally reflects heavier Put writing relative to Call writing. Since option writers typically sell options they expect to expire worthless, a PCR in this range is conventionally read as a bullish-leaning signal, with Put sellers positioning for SENSEX to hold above their strikes rather than expecting a sharp decline. It also continues the climb from 1.06 on the previous SENSEX expiry, and sits above the 1.60 seen two expiries ago.

That said, PCR is one data point among several, and a single week's reading should be considered alongside price action and the broader OI structure rather than in isolation.
