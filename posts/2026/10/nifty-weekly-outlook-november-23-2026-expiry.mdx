## Open Interest (OI) Analysis

Total Call OI stands at 1,289.28 lakh contracts against Total Put OI of 1,400.22 lakh contracts, with Put OI running modestly ahead of Call OI for this expiry. If you're not familiar with how Open Interest is built up and interpreted, [What Is Open Interest? A Beginner's Guide to OI in Options Trading](https://www.disciplinetrading.com/blog/what-is-open-interest) covers the basics.

The OI distribution across strikes shows clear concentration zones on both sides:

- The heaviest Put OI on the entire chain, by a wide margin, sits at the 25,000 strike, well below current spot, making it the primary support zone for the week. Secondary Put clusters appear at 25,300, 25,200, 25,400 and 25,100.
- The heaviest Call OI is concentrated at the 25,500 strike, just above current spot, marking the primary resistance zone, with a sizeable Put cluster alongside it making 25,500 an active pivot. A further Call concentration sits right at spot near 25,400.
- Beyond the pivot, Call OI is spread across 25,700, 25,800 and 25,900, forming a broader resistance layer above.

## Change in OI Analysis

Looking at fresh Open Interest additions for the day rather than the cumulative total gives a read on where positioning is actively building right now.

Fresh writing is heaviest at the 25,400 and 25,500 strikes on both sides, right around current spot, reinforcing that band as the key zone to watch this week. Further fresh Call additions appear at 25,800, 25,900 and 25,600, while fresh Put additions appear at 25,100 and just above spot near 25,450. Call unwinding is visible at 25,200 and 25,300.

This lines up with the cumulative OI picture: option writers are converging on the 25,400–25,500 band as the pivotal area for the week, with Call positions below spot being unwound while new Call writing builds further out.

## Put-Call Ratio (PCR) Analysis

The current PCR for this expiry is 1.08, meaning Put OI and Call OI are close to evenly matched, with Puts marginally ahead.

A PCR sitting close to 1 generally reflects a fairly balanced options market, without a strong directional lean from option writers on either side. This is a more neutral reading than a PCR skewed well above or below 1, and marks a slight cooling from the 1.13 reading on the previous NIFTY expiry.

That said, PCR is one data point among several, and a single week's reading should be considered alongside price action and the broader OI structure rather than in isolation.
